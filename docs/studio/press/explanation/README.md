> *Understanding-oriented.* Why one engine is sold through three doors.

## One engine, three buyers

Developers, agencies, and SMBs all need content sites, but they buy very differently:

- **Developers** want control and zero cost → **Open Source**, self-hosted (`@nis/create`).
- **Agencies** manage many client sites and want the plumbing handled → **Cloud**, a managed
  multi-tenant platform (`studio-press-cloud`) with consolidated billing and a portfolio dashboard.
- **SMBs** don't want to touch tooling at all → **Co.**, where [Studio Co.](../../co/) operates the
  Press for them (done-for-you).

Building three separate products for these buyers would fragment effort. Instead, one **engine**
(the Press CMS core) is packaged three ways. Pinned versions across generated sites make the Cloud
and Co. fleets maintainable — the same property that makes self-host upgrades coordinated.

## Why this matters to the umbrella

Press is the clearest example of the OGS model: **OGS Engine builds the core; OGS Studio packages
and sells it.** The doors are pure go-to-market — the technology underneath is shared. See
[How it connects](../../../explanation/how-it-connects.md).
